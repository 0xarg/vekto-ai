## The challenge

A global freight and logistics operator ran its partner-facing order, booking
and shipment-tracking integrations on TIBCO BusinessWorks — 134 flows
connecting carriers, customs brokers and warehouse systems across four regions.

With BW5 approaching end of support and license costs rising, the company
needed to move to Azure Logic Apps to stay inside its existing Microsoft
agreement. Two manual-migration quotes from systems integrators came back at 14
months and roughly $1.6M. That was a timeline the business could not accept,
given contractual SLAs with carrier partners that could not tolerate downtime
during cutover.

## Why VektoForge

The team had already lived through one stalled modernization attempt on a
smaller estate, and was wary of another open-ended rebuild.

VektoForge's fixed-scope model — a measured assessment before any commitment,
followed by a complete, tested project — matched what the business actually
needed: a known cost, a known timeline, and confidence that fifteen years of
accumulated shipment-routing logic would not be silently reinterpreted in the
process.

## What happened

The engagement opened with a free assessment. VektoForge measured the full
134-flow estate and converted a sample set of the customer's own workflows as
proof, before any contract was signed.

Full conversion followed, producing deployable Logic Apps projects,
transformation logic and generated tests for every flow. The resulting gap
report flagged 11 of 134 flows for manual review — primarily custom
error-handling patterns, and two integrations with undocumented
carrier-specific business rules.

The customer's own integration team closed those gaps over five weeks, while
VektoForge's delivery layer ran parallel validation against the legacy system's
output, flow by flow, before cutover.

## Why it worked

1. **Fixed scope from a measured assessment**, not an estimate.
2. **Sample conversion proved accuracy** before commitment.
3. **Deterministic output made carrier-logic review tractable** — the 11 flagged
   flows were reviewable by the customer's own team rather than opaque.
