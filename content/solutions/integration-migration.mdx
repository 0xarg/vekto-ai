The most disciplined version of a migration is also the least glamorous: move
what exists, preserve what it does, and change nothing else until it runs.

The discipline pays off at the point of failure. When behaviour preservation is
the goal, any difference between old and new is a defect with an obvious owner.
When migration and redesign happen together, every difference needs
investigating before anyone can say whether it was intended.

## What the programme involves

1. **Inventory and dependency mapping** across the source estate.
2. **Classification** — what maps cleanly, what needs restructuring, and what has
   no equivalent on the target and requires a human decision.
3. **Generation** of target-platform implementations following the mapping.
4. **Comparison** against source behaviour, with every difference surfaced and
   ranked rather than discovered later.
5. **Handover** with the record of how each artefact was treated.

## What this does not solve

Like-for-like migration reproduces the estate you have, including the parts you
would rather not have. That is the point — but it means the backlog of things
worth improving survives the move. Knowing precisely what is in that backlog,
which the inventory gives you, is usually the more valuable outcome.
